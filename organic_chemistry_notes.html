Organic Chemistry: Functional Groups, Mechanisms, Conversions & Chemical Tests

Module 1: Hydrocarbons, Haloalkanes & Haloarenes

1. Preparation & Reactions of Alkyl Halides ($\text{R-X}$)

  • From Alcohols (Halogenation):
    • $\text{PCl}_3, \text{PBr}_3, \text{PI}_3$ Reagent #1: Converts alcohols into alkyl halides.
    • $\text{SOCl}_2, \text{PCl}_5$ Reagent #2: Converts alcohols into alkyl chlorides (and carboxylic acids into acid chlorides).
    • $\text{HCl}/\text{ZnCl}_2, \text{HBr}, \text{HI}$ Reagent #3: Converts alcohols into alkyl halides.
  • From Alkanes / Alkyl Benzenes (Free Radical Halogenation):
    • $\text{Cl}_2/h\nu$ Reagent #51: Chlorination on the alkyl group of benzene or alkane.
  • Halogen Exchange Name Reactions:
    • Finkelstein Reaction (Name Rxn #1): $\text{CH}_3\text{Br} + \text{NaI} \xrightarrow{\text{Acetone}} \text{CH}_3\text{I} + \text{NaBr}$.
    • Swarts Reaction (Name Rxn #2 / Reagent #8): Uses $\text{AgF}, \text{Hg}_2\text{F}_2, \text{SbF}_3$, or $\text{CoF}_2$ to convert alkyl halides into alkyl fluorides ($\text{CH}_3\text{Br} + \text{AgF} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{F} + \text{AgBr}$).
  • Substitution, Elimination & Organometallic Pathway (Scheme I — Conversions of Alkyl Halides $\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}$):
    • $\text{Aq. KOH}$ Reagent #19a / Scheme I: Substitutes halide to convert alkyl halide into alcohol ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$), which oxidizes via $\text{Cu}/573\text{ K}$ ($-\text{H}_2$) to aldehyde ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CHO}$) and via $\text{KMnO}_4$ to carboxylic acid ($\text{RCH}_2\text{COOH}$) (or directly from alcohol to carboxylic acid via $\text{KMnO}_4$).
    • $\text{Alcoholic KOH}$ Reagent #29b / Scheme I: Eliminates $\text{HX}$ to convert alkyl halide into alkene ($\text{RCH=CH}_2$). Adding $\text{Br}_2$ gives $\text{RCHBr-CH}_2\text{Br}$, which with $\text{alc. KOH}$ ($-2\text{KBr}, -2\text{H}_2\text{O}$) yields an alkyne ($\text{RC}\equiv\text{CH}$).
    • $\text{NH}_3$ Reagent #84 / Scheme I: Ammonolysis ($-\text{HCl}$) converts alkyl halides into alkyl amines ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$).
    • $\text{Na}/\text{dry ether}$ — Wurtz Reaction (Reagent #9 / Name Rxn #5 / Scheme I): Couples alkyl halides into symmetrical alkanes with double the carbons ($\text{H}_3\text{C-Cl} + 2\text{Na} + \text{Cl-CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{Dry ether}} \text{H}_3\text{C-CH}_3 + 2\text{NaCl}$, or $2\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + 2\text{Na} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{R}$).
    • $\text{Mg, ether}$ (Grignard Formation — Scheme I): Converts $\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}$ into $\text{R-CH}_2\text{MgBr}$, which reacts with:
      • $\text{HCHO}$ to give a $1^\circ$ alcohol ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$).
      • $\text{R'CHO}$ to give a $2^\circ$ alcohol ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH(OH)R'}$).
      • $\text{R'COR''}$ to give a $3^\circ$ alcohol ($\text{RCH}_2\text{C(OH)(R')R''}$).
      • $\text{CO}_2$ to give a carboxylic acid ($\text{RCH}_2\text{COOH}$).

2. Benzene Electrophilic Substitution & Haloarenes (Scheme II)

  • Direct Electrophilic Substitution on Benzene:
    • Chlorination Reagent #4 & #12: $\text{Cl}_2/\text{Fe}$ or $\text{Cl}_2/\text{FeCl}_3$ substitutes a $-\text{Cl}$ group on benzene.
    • Bromination Reagent #11: $\text{Br}_2/\text{FeBr}_3$ brominates benzene.
    • Nitration Reagent #15: $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4/\text{HNO}_3$ nitrates benzene.
    • Sulphonation Reagent #17: $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ sulphonates benzene.
    • Friedel-Crafts Alkylation (Reagent #13 / Name Rxn #3): $\text{Benzene} + \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhydrous AlCl}_3} \text{Toluene}$ (alkylation of benzene and its derivatives).
    • Friedel-Crafts Acylation (Reagent #14 / Name Rxn #4): $\text{Benzene} + \text{CH}_3\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhydrous AlCl}_3} \text{Acetophenone}$ (acylation on benzene).
  • Reactions of Aryl Halides / Chlorobenzene (Scheme II):
    • Dow's Process to Phenol (Reagent #10 / Scheme II): $\text{NaOH}$ at $623\text{ K}, 300\text{ atm}$ (also listed as $623/443/368\text{ K}$) converts chlorobenzene into sodium phenoxide ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa}$), which upon acidification with $\text{dil. HCl}$ yields Phenol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$).
    • To Aryl Amine (Aniline): $2\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + 2\text{NH}_3 + \text{Cu}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$.
    • To Cyanobenzene: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl/Br} + \text{CuCN} \xrightarrow{\text{Pyridine, } 475\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CN} + \text{CuBr}$.
    • To Organometallics:
      • $\text{Mg, ether} \rightarrow \text{Phenyl magnesium bromide } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{MgBr})$.
      • $2\text{Li, dry ether} \rightarrow \text{Phenyl lithium } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Li}) + \text{LiBr}$.
    • Wurtz-Fittig Reaction (Name Rxn #7 / Scheme II): $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{Cl-CH}_3 + 2\text{Na} \xrightarrow{\text{Dry ether}} \text{Toluene } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3) + 2\text{NaCl}$.
    • Fittig Reaction (Name Rxn #6 / Scheme II): $2\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + 2\text{Na} \xrightarrow{\text{Dry ether}} \text{Diphenyl } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-C}_6\text{H}_5) + 2\text{NaCl}$.
    • Reduction to Benzene: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + 2\text{H} \xrightarrow{\text{Ni-Al / NaOH}} \text{Benzene } (\text{C}_6\text{H}_6) + \text{HCl}$.
    • Ring Substitution on Chlorobenzene:
      • $\text{Cl}_2 \rightarrow o\text{-dichlorobenzene} + p\text{-dichlorobenzene}$.
      • $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4, \Delta \rightarrow o\text{-chlorobenzenesulphonic acid} + p\text{-chlorobenzenesulphonic acid}$.
Module 2: Alcohols, Phenols & Ethers

1. Preparation & Reactions of Alcohols ($\text{R-OH}$) (Scheme III)

  • Industrial & Fermentation Synthesis:
    • $\text{Zn}/\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3$ at $200\text{--}300\text{ atm}, 573\text{--}673\text{ K}$ Reagent #40: Converts $\text{CO}$ and $\text{H}_2$ into methanol.
    • Invertase Reagent #41: Converts sucrose into glucose or fructose.
    • Zymase Reagent #42: Converts glucose or fructose into ethanol.
  • Hydration of Alkenes:
    • $\text{H}_2\text{O}/\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ Reagent #18: Acid-catalyzed hydration of alkenes into alcohols.
    • $\text{BH}_3/\text{H}_2\text{O}_2/\text{OH}^-$ Reagent #19b: Hydroboration-oxidation of alkenes into alcohols giving the Anti-Markownikoff product.
  • Dehydration of Alcohols (Temperature & Acid Strength Pattern):
    • $1^\circ\text{ Alcohol} + \text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4$ at $443\text{ K}$ ($\Delta$) Reagent #26 / Scheme III: Dehydrates primary alcohols into alkenes ($\text{RCH=CH}_2$), which can be hydrogenated via $\text{H}_2/\text{Ni}, \Delta$ to alkanes ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_3$) or brominated ($\text{Br}_2$) and treated with $\text{alc. KOH}$ to form alkynes ($\text{RC}\equiv\text{CH}$).
    • $\text{Alcohol} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ at $410\text{ K}$ Reagent #27: Bimolecular dehydration of alcohols into ethers.
    • $2^\circ\text{ Alcohol} + 85\%\text{ H}_3\text{PO}_4$ at $440\text{ K}$ Reagent #28: Dehydrates secondary alcohol into alkene.
    • $3^\circ\text{ Alcohol} + 20\%\text{ H}_3\text{PO}_4$ at $358\text{ K}$ Reagent #29a: Dehydrates tertiary alcohol into alkene.
  • Oxidation & Dehydrogenation of Alcohols:
    • $\text{PCC}$ Reagent #44: Mild oxidation of alcohol to aldehyde.
    • $\text{CrO}_3/\text{KMnO}_4$ or $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ in acidic medium Reagent #30 / Scheme III: Strong oxidation of alcohols directly into carboxylic acids ($\text{RCH}_2\text{COOH}$) (or oxidizes the intermediate aldehyde $\text{RCH}_2\text{CHO}$ via $\text{KMnO}_4$ or $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ to $\text{RCH}_2\text{COOH}$).
    • $\text{Cu}/573\text{ K}$ Reagent #31 / Scheme III: Catalytic dehydrogenation ($-\text{H}_2$) converts $1^\circ$ alcohols into aldehydes, $2^\circ$ alcohols into ketones, and $3^\circ$ alcohols into alkenes.
  • Other Reactions of Alcohols (Scheme III):
    • $\text{Na}$ metal Reagent #24: Converts alcohol or phenol into sodium alkoxide/phenoxide.
    • $\text{PCl}_5$ (Scheme III): Converts $\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$ into alkyl halide ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}$), which with $\text{NH}_3$ ($-\text{HCl}$) gives $\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$.
    • $\text{NH}_3\text{ (vap)}/\Delta/\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ (Scheme III): Directly converts alcohol ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$) into alkyl amine ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$).

2. Preparation & Reactions of Phenols ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$) (Schemes IV & V)

  • Preparation from Cumene: $\text{O}_2/\text{H}^+$ Reagent #23 converts cumene into phenol.
  • Acid-Base, Reduction & Amine Reactions (Scheme IV):
    • $\text{2Na}$ or $\text{NaOH}$ Reagent #24 / Scheme IV: Forms sodium phenoxide ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{ONa}$) plus $\text{H}_2$ (with $\text{Na}$) or $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (with $\text{NaOH}$).
    • $\text{Zn}$ dust Reagent #38 / Scheme IV: Reduces phenol into benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{ZnO}$).
    • $\text{NH}_3, \text{Anhyd. ZnCl}_2, 675\text{ K}$ (Scheme IV): Converts phenol into aniline ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$).
    • $3\text{H}_2/\text{Ni}$ (Scheme V): Hydrogenates phenol into cyclohexanol.
    • $\text{CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2$ or $\text{Na}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7/\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or air Reagent #39 / Scheme V: Oxidizes phenol into $p$-benzoquinone.
  • Acylation, Fries Rearrangement & Alkylation (Schemes IV & V):
    • $\text{CH}_3\text{COCl}$ or $(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O}$ in Pyridine / $\text{Conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4$ / $\text{AlCl}_3$ Reagent #16 & #25 / Scheme IV: Performs O-acylation of phenol ($-\text{HCl}$) to form phenyl acetate ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OCOCH}_3$), which undergoes rearrangement with $\text{AlCl}_3, \Delta$ to yield $o$-hydroxyacetophenone ($o$-phenyl acetate isomer) and $p$-hydroxyacetophenone.
    • $\text{CH}_3\text{Cl} / \text{Anhyd. AlCl}_3$ (Scheme V): Friedel-Crafts alkylation of phenol gives $o$-cresol and $p$-cresol ($+\text{HCl}$).
  • Electrophilic Ring Substitution on Phenol (Solvent & Temperature Effects — Scheme IV & V):
    • Bromination:
      • $\text{Br}_2/\text{CS}_2$ Reagent #35 / Scheme IV: Non-polar solvent gives mono-bromination ($o$-bromophenol + $p$-bromophenol).
      • $\text{Br}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}$ Reagent #34 / Scheme IV: Polar medium gives tri-bromination (2,4,6-tribromophenol + $3\text{HBr}$).
    • Nitration:
      • $\text{Dil. HNO}_3$ Reagent #32 / Scheme IV: Gives mono-nitration ($o$-nitrophenol + $p$-nitrophenol).
      • $\text{Conc. HNO}_3 + \text{Conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4$ Reagent #33 / Scheme V: Gives tri-nitration (2,4,6-trinitrophenol / Picric acid + $3\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
    • Sulphonation:
      • $\text{Conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4$ at $288\text{--}293\text{ K}$ (Scheme IV): Forms 2-hydroxybenzenesulphonic acid ($o$-isomer).
      • $\text{Conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4$ at $373\text{ K}$ (Scheme IV): Forms 4-hydroxybenzenesulphonic acid ($p$-isomer).
  • Special Name Reactions of Phenol (Scheme V):
    • Kolbe's Reaction (Reagent #36 / Name Rxn #8 / Scheme V): $\text{Phenol} \xrightarrow{\text{NaOH, CO}_2\text{ (4–7 atm), H}^+} \text{Salicylic acid}$ ($o$-hydroxybenzoic acid).
      • Aspirin Synthesis (Scheme V): Treating salicylic acid with $(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O} / \text{Conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4$ yields Aspirin (2-acetoxybenzoic acid + $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$).
    • Reimer-Tiemann Reaction (Reagent #37 / Name Rxn #9 / Scheme V):
      • With $\text{CHCl}_3 + \text{NaOH}$ ($340\text{ K}$), then $\text{HCl}$: Converts phenol into Salicylaldehyde.
      • With $\text{CCl}_4 + \text{NaOH}$ ($340\text{ K}$), then $\text{HCl}$: Converts phenol into Salicylic acid.
    • Coupling Reaction (Name Rxn #28 / Scheme V): $\text{Phenol} + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{NaOH / OH}^-} p\text{-Hydroxyazobenzene} + \text{HCl}$.
    • $\text{FeCl}_3$ Reaction (Scheme V): $3\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{FeCl}_3 \rightarrow (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O})_3\text{Fe}$ (Ferric phenoxide, violet color) + $3\text{HCl}$.

3. Preparation & Reactions of Ethers ($\text{R-O-R'}$)

  • Williamson Synthesis (Name Rxn #10): $\text{CH}_3\text{-Br} + \text{CH}_3\text{-ONa} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{-O-CH}_3 + \text{NaBr}$.
  • Cleavage by $\text{HI}$ Reagent #43: Converts ether into alcohol and alkyl halide.
Module 3: Aldehydes & Ketones (Carbonyl Compounds)

1. Preparation of Aldehydes & Ketones

  • Aldehydes Only:
    • Rosenmund Reduction (Reagent #45 / Name Rxn #14): $\text{R-COCl} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pd/BaSO}_4} \text{R-CHO}$ (acid chloride into aldehyde; also shown in Scheme VI-Carboxylic Acids as $\text{H}_2/\text{Ni, Quinoline}$).
    • Stephen Reaction (Reagent #46 / Name Rxn #11): $\text{R-CN} + \text{SnCl}_2 + \text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{R-CH=NH} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{R-CHO}$ (cyanides into aldehydes).
    • DIBAL-H Reduction Reagent #47 & #48: $\text{AlH(i-Bu)}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (DIBAL-H) selectively reduces cyanides into aldehydes (#47) and esters into aldehydes (#48).
    • Etard Reaction (Reagent #49 / Name Rxn #12): $\text{Toluene} \xrightarrow{\text{i) CrO}_2\text{Cl}_2\text{ ii) H}_2\text{O}} \text{Benzaldehyde}$.
    • Chromic Oxide + Acetic Anhydride Reagent #50: $\text{CrO}_3/(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O}$ converts toluene into benzaldehyde.
    • Gattermann-Koch Reaction (Reagent #52 / Name Rxn #13): $\text{Benzene} \xrightarrow{\text{CO, HCl, Anhydrous AlCl}_3} \text{Benzaldehyde}$.
  • Ketones Only:
    • Dialkyl Cadmium Reagent #53: $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{Cd}$ converts acid chlorides into ketones.
    • Grignard on Nitriles Reagent #54: $\text{RMgX}/\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ converts cyanides into ketones.

2. Nucleophilic Addition & Condensation on Carbonyls ($>\text{C=O}$)

  • Direct Additions:
    • $\text{HCN}$ Reagent #55: Converts carbonyl compound into cyanohydrin.
    • $\text{NaHSO}_3$ Reagent #56: Forms bisulphite addition product with aldehydes and ketones.
    • $\text{RMgX}/\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ Reagent #22: Converts aldehydes and ketones into alcohols.
    • $\text{ROH}/\text{HCl}$ Reagent #62: Converts aldehydes and ketones into hemiacetal and acetal.
    • $\text{HO-CH}_2\text{-CH}_2\text{-OH}/\text{HCl}$ Reagent #63: Converts aldehyde or ketone into ethylene glycol ketal.
  • Addition-Elimination with Ammonia Derivatives ($\text{H}_2\text{N-Z}$):
    • $\text{H}_2\text{NOH}$ (Hydroxylamine) Reagent #57: Carbonyl compound $\rightarrow$ oxime.
    • $\text{H}_2\text{N-NH}_2$ (Hydrazine) Reagent #58: Carbonyl compound $\rightarrow$ hydrazone.
    • $\text{H}_2\text{N-NH-Ph}$ (Phenylhydrazine) Reagent #59: Carbonyl compound $\rightarrow$ phenylhydrazone.
    • $\text{2,4-DNP}$ Reagent #60: Carbonyl compound $\rightarrow$ 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone (orange ppt).
    • $\text{H}_2\text{N-NH-CO-CH}_3$ / Semicarbazide Reagent #61: Carbonyl compound $\rightarrow$ semicarbazone (written as semicarbazide in table).
    • $\text{R'CHO} + \text{RCH}_2\text{NH}_2$ (Scheme VII): Forms Schiff's base ($\text{RCH=NR'}$).

3. Reduction, Oxidation & Alpha-Hydrogen Reactions (Scheme VI — Aldehydes)

  • Reduction to Alcohols:
    • $\text{NaBH}_4 / \text{LiAlH}_4$ (LAH) Reagent #20: Reduces aldehydes, ketones, and acids into alcohols (and nitro, cyanides, isocyanides into amines).
    • $\text{H}_2/\text{Ni}$ or $\text{H}_2/\text{Pd}$ Reagent #21 / Scheme VI-Aldehydes: Reduces aldehydes and ketones into alcohols ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$) (and reduces cyanides). From alcohol ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$), Scheme VI shows the standard branch: $\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4, \Delta \rightarrow \text{RCH=CH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2} \text{RCHBr-CH}_2\text{Br} \xrightarrow{\text{alc. KOH}} \text{RC}\equiv\text{CH}$ (alkyne), or $\text{PCl}_5 \rightarrow \text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{NH}_3, -\text{HCl}} \text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$.
  • Complete Deoxygenation to Alkanes ($>\text{C=O} \rightarrow -\text{CH}_2-$):
    • Clemmensen Reduction (Reagent #64 / Name Rxn #15 / Scheme VI): $\text{Zn-Hg}/\text{Conc. HCl}$ reduces carbonyl compounds (aldehydes/ketones) into alkanes ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_3$).
    • Wolff-Kishner Reduction (Reagent #65 / Name Rxn #16 / Scheme VI): $\text{NH}_2\text{-NH}_2$ followed by $\text{KOH}/\text{ethylene glycol}$ reduces carbonyl compounds into alkanes ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_3$).
  • Oxidation of Carbonyls:
    • $\text{Aldehydes} + \text{KMnO}_4$ (Scheme VI-Aldehydes): Oxidizes $\text{RCH}_2\text{CHO}$ into carboxylic acid ($\text{RCH}_2\text{COOH}$).
    • $\text{Ketones} + \text{KMnO}_4/\text{OH}^-/\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7/\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or $\text{HNO}_3$ Reagent #66: Prolonged oxidation cleaves ketones into a mixture of carboxylic acids.
    • Tollens' Test (Reagent #67 / Name Rxn #17): $\text{R-CHO} + 2[\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]^+ + 3\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{R-COO}^- + 2\text{Ag}\downarrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 4\text{NH}_3$ (using $[\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]\text{NO}_3 + \text{NaOH}$).
    • Fehling's Test (Reagent #68 / Name Rxn #18): $\text{R-CHO} + 2\text{Cu}^{2+} + 5\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{R-COO}^- + \text{Cu}_2\text{O}\downarrow + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (using $\text{Cu(OH)}_2$).
    • Iodoform Reaction (Reagent #69 / Name Rxn #19): $\text{H}_3\text{C-CO-CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{I}_2/\text{NaOH or NaOI}} \text{CHI}_3\downarrow + \text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$.
  • Base-Catalyzed Disproportionation & Condensation ($\alpha$-H Logic):
    • Aldol Condensation (Reagent #70 / Name Rxn #20 — Requires $\alpha$-H): $2\text{H}_3\text{C-CHO} \xrightarrow{\text{dil. NaOH or Ba(OH)}_2} \text{H}_3\text{C-CH(OH)-CH}_2\text{-CHO} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CH}_3\text{-CH=CH-CHO}$.
    • Cannizzaro Reaction (Reagent #71 / Name Rxn #21 — No $\alpha$-H): $\text{HCHO} + \text{HCHO} \xrightarrow{\text{Conc. KOH or NaOH}} \text{HCOONa} + \text{H}_3\text{C-OH}$.
Module 4: Carboxylic Acids & Derivatives ($\text{R-COOH}$) (Scheme VI — Carboxylic Acids)
  • Preparation of Carboxylic Acids:
    • Alkyl Benzene Oxidation Reagent #72: $\text{KMnO}_4/\text{KOH}$ oxidizes toluene or alkyl benzene into Benzoic Acid.
    • Acid Hydrolysis of Derivatives ($\text{H}_2\text{O}/\text{H}^+$) Reagent #73: Hydrolyzes cyanides into carboxylic acids, amides into carboxylic acids, esters into carboxylic acids and alcohols, and acid chlorides or anhydrides into carboxylic acids.
  • Conversion into Acid Derivatives (Acyl Nucleophilic Substitution):
    • To Acid Chlorides ($\text{RCH}_2\text{COCl}$) Reagent #2 & #78 / Scheme VI: Uses $\text{PCl}_3, \text{PCl}_5$, or $\text{SOCl}_2$.
    • To Esters ($\text{RCH}_2\text{COOR'}$) Reagent #77 / Scheme VI: Esterification using $\text{R'OH}/\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4$.
      • Saponification / Salt Formation Reagent #74: $\text{NaOH}$ saponifies esters and converts acids into the salt of the acid.
    • To Amides ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CONH}_2$) Reagent #79 / Scheme VI: Reaction with $\text{NH}_3$ and heating (or treating $\text{RCH}_2\text{COCl}$ with $\text{NH}_3$).
    • To Acid Anhydrides (or Nitriles) Reagent #76: $\text{P}_4\text{O}_{10}$ or $\text{P}_2\text{O}_5$ dehydrates carboxylic acids into anhydrides (and dehydrates amides into nitriles).
  • Reduction, Decarboxylation & $\alpha$-Halogenation:
    • To Alcohols ($\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$) Reagent #20 & #81 / Scheme VI: Uses $\text{LiAlH}_4$ or $\text{B}_2\text{H}_6$ (Note: $\text{LiAlH}_4$ also reduces amides into amines).
    • To Alkanes (Same Carbon Count — $\text{RCH}_2\text{CH}_3$) (Scheme VI): Uses $\text{HI}/\text{Red P}$ (also listed alongside Clemmensen $\text{Zn(Hg)}+\text{HCl}$ or Wolff-Kishner $\text{NH}_2\text{-NH}_2+\text{KOH}+\text{glycol}$ in Scheme VI).
    • To Alkanes (One Carbon Less — Decarboxylation) Reagent #80 / Scheme VI: $\text{NaOH}/\text{CaO}, \Delta$ (soda-lime) converts $\text{RCH}_2\text{COOH}$ into $\text{RCH}_3$.
    • To Amines (One Carbon Less) (Scheme VI): $\text{RCH}_2\text{CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2/\text{KOH (Hoffmann Bromamide)}} \text{RCH}_2\text{NH}_2$.
    • Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) Reaction (Reagent #82 / Name Rxn #22): $\text{H}_3\text{C-COOH} \xrightarrow{\text{i) Cl}_2/\text{Red P (or P}_4\text{) ii) H}_2\text{O}} \text{Cl-CH}_2\text{-COOH}$ ($\alpha$-halogenation of carboxylic acids).
    • Bicarbonate Test Reagent #75: $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ or $\text{NaHCO}_3$ tests for carboxylic acids ($\text{CO}_2$ effervescence).
Module 5: Nitrogen Compounds (Amines, Diazonium Salts, Cyanides & Nitro)

1. Preparation of Amines

  • Reduction of Nitro Compounds ($\text{R-NO}_2$) Reagent #20 & #83: Uses $\text{Sn}/\text{HCl}, \text{Fe}/\text{HCl}, \text{H}_2/\text{Pd}$, or $\text{LiAlH}_4$.
  • Reduction of Cyanides, Isocyanides & Amides Reagent #20, #21, #81, #85: Uses $\text{LiAlH}_4, \text{H}_2/\text{Ni}$, or $\text{H}_2/\text{Pd}$ (Note: Reagent #85 also notes amides into cyanides/amines context).
  • Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis (Reagent #86): $\text{KOH}/\text{R-X}$ converts phthalimide into primary aliphatic amine.
  • Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation (Reagent #87 / Name Rxn #23): $\text{R-CONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{R-NH}_2$ (converts amide into primary amine with one carbon less).

2. Reactions of Alkyl Amines ($\text{RCH}_2\text{NH}_2$) (Scheme VII)

  • Exhaustive Alkylation: $+ 4\text{RI} \rightarrow \text{R}_4\text{CH}_2\text{N}^+\text{I}^-$ (tetraalkylammonium iodide).
  • Acylation (N-Acylation) Reagent #25 / Scheme VII:
    • $+\text{R'COCl} \rightarrow \text{RCH}_2\text{NHCOR'}$ (amide).
    • $+(\text{R'CO})_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{RCH}_2\text{NHCOR'}$ (acid amide) $+ \text{R'COOH}$ (carboxylic acid).
    • $+\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCl}$ (Benzoyl chloride) $\rightarrow \text{RCH}_2\text{NHCOC}_6\text{H}_5$ ($N$-alkylbenzamide).
  • Schiff's Base Formation: $+\text{R'CHO} \rightarrow \text{RCH=NR'}$ (Schiff's base).
  • Carbylamine Reaction (Reagent #88 / Name Rxn #24 / Scheme VII): $\text{R-NH}_2 + \text{CHCl}_3 + 3\text{KOH (alc.)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{R-NC}$ (isocyanide, offensive/pungent smell) $+ 3\text{KCl} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
  • Reaction with Nitrous Acid ($\text{HNO}_2$ or $\text{NaNO}_2/\text{HCl}$) Reagent #89 / Scheme VII: Converts $1^\circ$ aliphatic amines ($\text{RCH}_2\text{NH}_2$) into alcohols ($\text{RCH}_2\text{OH}$) with evolution of $\text{N}_2$.
  • Hoffmann Mustard Oil Type Reaction (with $\text{CS}_2$) (Scheme VII): $\text{RCH}_2\text{NH}_2 + \text{S=C=S} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{RCH}_2\text{NH-CS-SH}$ (dithioalkyl carbamic acid) $\xrightarrow{\text{HgCl}_2, \text{ Heat}} \text{RCH}_2\text{-N=C=S}$ (alkyl isothiocyanate).
  • Reaction with Phosgene ($\text{Cl-CO-Cl}$) (Scheme VII): Yields symmetrical disubstituted urea ($\text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH-CO-NH-CH}_2\text{-R}$).
  • Oxidation with $\text{alk. KMnO}_4$ ($[\text{O}]$) (Scheme VII): $\text{RCH}_2\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{[\text{O}]} \text{RCH}_2\text{NHOH}$ (hydroxylamine) $\xrightarrow{[\text{O}]} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-N=O}$ (nitroso compound) $\xrightarrow{[\text{O}]} \text{R-CH}_2\text{NO}_2$ (nitro compound).
  • Hinsberg's Reagent Reagent #91: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}$ (benzenesulphonyl chloride) distinguishes $1^\circ, 2^\circ$, and $3^\circ$ amines.

3. Reactions of Aniline & Benzene Diazonium Chloride (Scheme VIII)

  • Electrophilic Substitution on Aniline:
    • Uncontrolled Bromination Reagent #92: $\text{Br}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}$ converts aniline into 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (white ppt).
    • Controlled Mono-Bromination via Protection Reagent #93: $\text{Br}_2 / \text{CH}_3\text{COCl}$ or $(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O}$ protects the $-\text{NH}_2$ group via N-acylation to convert aniline into bromoaniline.
  • Diazotisation (Reagent #5 & #90 / Name Rxn #25 / Scheme VIII): $\text{Ar-NH}_2$ (Aniline) $+ \text{NaNO}_2 + \text{dil. HCl} \xrightarrow{0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C } (273\text{--}278\text{ K})} \text{Ar-N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$ (Benzene diazonium chloride).
  • Reactions of Diazonium Chloride ($\text{Ar-N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$) Reagents #6 & #7 / Scheme VIII:
    • Sandmeyer Reaction (Reagent #6 / Name Rxn #26 / Scheme VIII):
      • $\text{CuCl}/\text{HCl}$ (or $\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2$) $\rightarrow \text{Ar-Cl}$ (Chlorobenzene) $+ \text{N}_2$.
      • $\text{CuBr}/\text{HBr}$ (or $\text{Cu}_2\text{Br}_2$) $\rightarrow \text{Ar-Br}$ (Bromobenzene) $+ \text{N}_2$.
      • $\text{CuCN} \rightarrow \text{Ar-CN}$ (Benzonitrile / Cyanobenzene) $+ \text{N}_2$.
    • Gattermann Reaction (Name Rxn #27): $\text{Cu}/\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{Chlorobenzene} + \text{N}_2$.
    • Iodination Reagent #6 / Scheme VIII: $\text{KI}, \Delta \rightarrow \text{Ar-I}$ (Iodobenzene).
    • Balz-Schiemann Fluorination (Reagent #7 / Scheme VIII): $\text{HBF}_4$ or $\text{NaBF}_4 \rightarrow \text{Ar-F}$ (Fluorobenzene).
    • Nitration (Scheme VIII): $\text{HBF}_4, \text{NaNO}_2 \rightarrow \text{Ar-NO}_2$ (Nitrobenzene).
    • Hydrolysis to Phenol Reagent #6 / Scheme VIII: $\text{H}_2\text{O}/\Delta \rightarrow \text{Ar-OH}$ (Phenol).
    • Reduction to Benzene Reagent #6 / Scheme VIII: $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2, \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Ar-H}$ (Benzene).
    • To Thiophenol (Scheme VIII): $\text{NaSH} \rightarrow \text{Ar-SH}$ (Thiophenol).
    • Azo Coupling (Name Rxn #28 / Scheme VIII): $+\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-R'}$ (e.g., Phenol or $\beta$-naphthol) $\rightarrow \text{Ar-N=N-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{-R'}$ (Azo dye).
Module 6: Carbon Chain Manipulation (Ascending & Descending Series)

1. Ascending the Series (Adding Carbon Atoms)

  • By Wurtz Reaction:
    • Symmetrical: $\text{R-X} + 2\text{Na} + \text{X-R} \xrightarrow{\text{Dry ether}} \text{R-R} + 2\text{NaX}$.
    • Unsymmetrical: $\text{R-X} + 2\text{Na} + \text{X-R'} \xrightarrow{\text{Dry ether}} \text{R-R'} + 2\text{NaX}$.
  • By Using Cyanide ($\text{KCN}$):
    • $\text{ROH} \xrightarrow{\text{Red P + I}_2} \text{RI} \xrightarrow{\text{KCN}} \text{RCN}$.
    • From $\text{RCN}$, reduce via $\text{H}_2/\text{Ni}$ or $\text{Na in C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$ to $\text{RCH}_2\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{HNO}_2} \text{RCH}_2\text{OH}$ (alcohol with one more carbon), which can be oxidized via $\text{alkaline KMnO}_4\text{ } [\text{O}]$ to $\text{RCHO}$ (and reduced back via $\text{H}_2/\text{Ni}$ to $\text{RCH}_2\text{OH}$). Alternatively, hydrolyze $\text{RCN}$ via $\text{H}_2\text{O}/\text{H}^+$ to $\text{RCOOH}$.
  • By Using Grignard's Reagent ($\text{RMgX}$):
    • $+\text{HCHO}$ (Formaldehyde) $\rightarrow \text{R-CH}_2\text{-OMgX} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2\text{O}, -\text{Mg(OH)X}} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-OH}$ ($1^\circ$ alcohol).
    • $+\text{R'CHO}$ (Aldehyde) $\rightarrow \text{R-CH(OMgX)-R'} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2\text{O}, -\text{Mg(OH)X}} \text{R-CH(OH)-R'}$ ($2^\circ$ alcohol).
    • $+\text{R'-CO-R''}$ (Ketone) $\rightarrow \text{R-C(OMgX)(R')-R''} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2\text{O}, -\text{Mg(OH)X}} \text{R-C(OH)(R')-R''}$ ($3^\circ$ alcohol).
  • By Using Sodium Alkynides (for terminal alkynes):
    • $\text{R-X} + \text{NaC}\equiv\text{C-R} \rightarrow \text{R-C}\equiv\text{C-R} + \text{NaX}$.

2. Descent of Series (Removing a Carbon Atom)

  • By Hoffmann Bromamide Reaction:
    • $\text{RCOOH} \xrightarrow{\text{NH}_3, \Delta} \text{RCONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2/\text{KOH (Hoffmann bromamide degradation)}} \text{RNH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{HNO}_2} \text{ROH} \xrightarrow{\text{alkaline KMnO}_4\text{ } [\text{O}]} \text{R'COOH}$.
  • By Decarboxylation Reaction:
    • $\text{RCOOH} \xrightarrow{\text{NaOH}} \text{RCOONa} \xrightarrow{\text{CaO + NaOH}} \text{R-H} \text{ (or }\text{R'CH}_3\text{)} \xrightarrow{\text{Cl}_2/h\nu} \text{R'CH}_2\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{aq. KOH}} \text{R'CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{alkaline KMnO}_4\text{ } [\text{O}]} \text{R'COOH}$.
Module 7: Chemical Tests to Distinguish Organic Compounds

1. The 12 Core Single-Test Rules

  1. Tollens' Test (Silver Mirror): Given by all aldehydes ($\text{R-CHO} + 2[\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]^+ + 3\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{RCOO}^- + 2\text{Ag}\downarrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 4\text{NH}_3$). Note: $\text{HCOOH}$ (methanoic acid) also gives this test; ketones ($\text{RCOR}$) do not.
  2. 2,4-DNP Test (Orange Ppt): Given by all aldehydes ($\text{R-CHO}$) and ketones ($\text{RCOR}$).
  3. Iodoform Test (Yellow Ppt of $\text{CHI}_3$): Given by aldehydes/ketones having a $\text{CH}_3\text{CO}-$ (keto methyl) group and alcohols having a $\text{CH}_3\text{CH(OH)}-$ group ($\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + 3\text{I}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{CHI}_3\downarrow + \text{HCOONa} + 3\text{NaI} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
  4. Specific Compounds Giving Positive Iodoform Test: Ethanol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$), propan-2-ol ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH(OH)CH}_3$), ethanal ($\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$), propanone ($\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3$), butanone ($\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_2\text{CH}_3$), pentan-2-one ($\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$), and acetophenone ($\text{PhCOCH}_3$).
  5. Bicarbonate Test (Effervescence): Given by all carboxylic acids ($\text{RCOOH} + \text{NaHCO}_3 \rightarrow \text{RCOONa} + \text{CO}_2\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}$).
  6. Neutral $\text{FeCl}_3$ Test (Violet Color): Given by phenol ($3\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{FeCl}_3 \rightarrow (\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O})_3\text{Fe} + 3\text{HCl}$).
  7. Carbylamine Test (Offensive Smell): Given by all primary amines, aliphatic or aromatic ($\text{R/Ar-NH}_2 + \text{CHCl}_3 + \text{KOH(alc)} \rightarrow \text{R-NC} + \text{KCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$).
  8. Azo Dye Test (Orange Dye): Given by aniline / aromatic primary amines only ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^-$, then add $\beta$-naphthol $\rightarrow$ orange dye).
  9. $\text{Na}$-Metal Test ($\text{H}_2$ Bubbles): Given by all alcohols ($\text{R-OH} + \text{Na} \rightarrow \text{R-ONa} + \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\uparrow$).
  10. Esters ($\text{RCOOR}$): Hydrolyze first, then test the resulting acid and alcohol products to identify them.
  11. Bromine Water Test: All alkenes ($\text{C=C}$) and alkynes ($\text{C}\equiv\text{C}$) decolorize $\text{Br}_2$-water from red to colorless.
  12. Lucas Test ($\text{ZnCl}_2/\text{HCl}$): Distinguishes $1^\circ, 2^\circ$, and $3^\circ$ alcohols:
    • $3^\circ$ alcohol: Immediate turbidity.
    • $2^\circ$ alcohol: Turbidity after some time (5–10 min).
    • $1^\circ$ alcohol: No turbidity at room temperature (appears only on heating).

c) Hydrolysis
d) Heating		 • $\text{R-CONH}_2$: No response to litmus; $-\text{ve}$ for Carbylamine; hydrolyzes ($\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2\text{O}$) to carboxylic acid ($\text{RCOOH}$); unaffected as isocyanide.
• $\text{R-NH}_2$: Turns red litmus blue; $+\text{ve}$ for Carbylamine (forms pungent $\text{R-NC}$). (Note: rows c and d in source Table XII also contrast Cyanide $\text{R-CN}$ vs Isocyanide $\text{R-NC}$: $\text{R-CN}$ hydrolyzes to $\text{RCONH}_2 \rightarrow \text{RCOOH}$ and has no effect on heating, whereas $\text{R-NC}$ hydrolyzes to $\text{RNH}_2 + \text{HCOOH}$ and rearranges to alkyl cyanide on heating).
XIII. Nitroalkane ($\text{R-NO}_2$) vs Alkyl Nitrite ($\text{R-O-N=O}$) a) Reduction ($\text{Sn}/\text{HCl}$) ($+6\text{H}$)
b) $\text{NaOH}$ Treatment		 • $\text{R-NO}_2$: (a) Reduces to primary amine ($\text{RNH}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$). (b) Forms a water-soluble sodium salt with $\text{NaOH}$.
• $\text{R-ONO}$: (a) Reduces to alcohol + ammonia ($\text{ROH} + \text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$). (b) Readily hydrolyzed by $\text{NaOH}$ into alcohol and sodium nitrite ($\text{ROH} + \text{NaNO}_2$).
XIV. Alkyl Cyanide ($\text{RCN}$) vs Alkyl Isocyanide ($\text{RNC}$) (plus Hydrolysis & Heating from Table XII c & d) a) Solubility in Water
b) Reduction ($+4\text{H}$) followed by $\text{HNO}_2$
c) Acid Hydrolysis (from Table XII c)
d) Heating (from Table XII d)		 • $\text{RCN}$ (Cyanide): (a) Soluble in water. (b) Reduces to $1^\circ$ amine ($\text{RCH}_2\text{NH}_2$), which with $\text{HNO}_2$ gives $\text{RCH}_2\text{OH} + \textbf{evolution of }\mathbf{\text{N}_2}$. (c) Hydrolyzes to amide ($\text{RCONH}_2$) then carboxylic acid ($\text{RCOOH}$). (d) No effect on heating.
• $\text{RNC}$ (Isocyanide): (a) Insoluble in water. (b) Reduces to $2^\circ$ methylamine ($\text{R-NH-CH}_3$), which with $\text{HNO}_2$ gives a yellow oily nitroso compound ($\text{R-N(CH}_3)\text{-N=O} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$). (c) Hydrolyzes ($+2\text{H}_2\text{O}$) to $1^\circ$ amine ($\text{RNH}_2$) + formic acid ($\text{HCOOH}$). (d) Rearranges to form alkyl cyanide ($\text{RCN}$) on heating.
